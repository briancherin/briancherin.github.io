As of July 2026, I potentially have a two week trip to Greece coming up in September. It barely crossed my mind to learn any of the language, until my mom made a comment: "Are you going to learn Greek?". I scoffed and said, "of course not." 

But the seed was planted. Once rooted in my mind, it started to feel inevitable for me to at least learn a little bit about the language and see what beginner resources are out there for learning it.

But still, I must address the question: Do I really need to learn any Greek?

Perhaps no -- English is pretty regularly used there, from what I've been reading. However, I think it vastly enriches the travel experience to learn at least just a little bit. 

At minimum, my goal is to learn some vocab and some very basic grammar. It would be nice to know the alphabet well enough to be able to at least read/pronounce signs and menu items, for example.

How far will I go into the language? I don't know. It might not be very far. But I'm viewing it as a mini language-learning project which probably will terminate after the Greece trip. Or, who knows, maybe I'll find myself want to go further with it even afterwards. (More likely though, I think there's a chance it will reinvigorate me back towards learning more Japanese and Spanish, which are my primary language learning goals at this time.)

#### Learning method
I took a glimpse at Duolingo. Two things drove me away:
1) The last time I used Duolingo was probably ten years ago when I was boosting my Spanish ability. I returned to the app now, and... my god - I did not like what I saw. Not only was I bugged by the constant Duoling-owl-as-superhero animations which add an annoying, forced pause between the too-short lessons, but I also quickly hit the free-tier daily limit of lessons I was able to complete. I don't recall such limits (or annoying animations) back when I was doing Spanish. I get it -- they've got to make money. But I'd call it an example of enshittification. Maybe some people are willing to deal with those things. But I could not. Why should I limit myself to a platform that wants to limit *my* learning progression (literally limiting the amount I can progress in a day).
2) Also, I saw people's opinions on Reddit that the Greek Duolingo course isn't very good. 

So, as of now, I have landed on a dual approach for my own "study" routine:
1) Anki vocab deck
2) Language Reactor audio course
