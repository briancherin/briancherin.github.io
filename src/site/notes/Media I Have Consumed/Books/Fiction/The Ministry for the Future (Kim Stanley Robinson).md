by [[Media I Have Consumed/Books/Authors/Kim Stanley Robinson\|Kim Stanley Robinson]]

___

'Twas an apocalyptic-feeling day - July 15, 2026 - when wildfire smoke that drifted from Canada had filled the NYC skies with a reddish haze. I was seventeen e-book pages away from completing [[Media I Have Consumed/Books/Fiction/War and Peace by Leo Tolstoy\|War and Peace]] and it would soon be time to select the next book I would read. After reading some Reddit comments in response to the wildfire events which made some dramatic (perhaps rightfully so) statements about climate change and humanity's future, something clicked in my mind and I knew it was time to read The Ministry for the Future. 

KSR is one of my favorite authors, so this book has been on my mind as one to read some time, and it always felt like an important one to get to. So, what better moment to start the book in than one where I was feeling a droplet of climate despair? It's kind of like listening to sad music when you're sad. There's a resonance between the moment and the media which makes it just feel right. Plus, I figured it might *frame* the moment for me - give me a framework of thought, a way to think about the situation, an outlook, maybe even an answer of whether hope or despair should win.

##### Initial thoughts up to chapter 44 (about a third through)
- Short chapters, I kind of like it. Many perspective shifts, but also we have recurring characters. We get to see the same characters through different lenses - one chapter may be first person for that character, and another may be third person from another person's perspective. This is definitely reminiscent of KSR's other works too.
- I can already feel it changing my outlook, mindset, thinking. Even though I know it's a fictional portrayal, I can feel that KSR is making it seem like a realistic scenario of the way things might happen in the next ten or so years (at least that's the timespan in the book so far). So it feels real and like the things might happen. For sure, the message is relevant to us now. That's clearly why KSR is writing this. The scene where Mary is confronted by Frank feels kind of like KSR shouting to the world, "What you're doing is not enough!". (I feel like that was one of the most impactful scenes of the book so far.) And that is starting to float in my mind too - what we're doing is not enough. It's troubling.
- There was a brief part in one of the interlude chapters (where it's not a particular character's perspective but more like a micro-essay about some relevant topic. Which, by the way, has been feeling very Tolstoyian (again, just finished War and Peace, where Tolstoy interleaved character/plot chapters with mini essays on the philosophy of history)) which talked about species that have gone extinct, and I found that I did not know if species have been going to extinct recently and if so, which ones, and this made me feel like I should keep up to date more with ecological (or just general environmental/science) news/events. [[Media I Have Consumed/Podcasts/Skeptics Guide to the Universe\|Skeptics Guide to the Universe]] podcast has been giving me a taste of science news updates, but they are just small tidbits. So I'm exploring some podcasts.:
	- *Outrage + Optimism: The Climate Podcast* - so far, listened to one episode and really liked it - it was a special Q&A episode, so I'm not sure yet what their normal episodes are like, but I liked the feeling of it. Definitely will listen to more.
	- *Living on Earth* - Also a climate change + ecology podcast. Listened to one episode so far, and I enjoyed the range of topics it covered and the interviews/clips with real people. But, it's a bit slow and it leans towards being less conversational and I did not find the host compelling. Will give it more chances though.
	- Other podcasts that I haven't tried out yet but that came up in my search^[search being a question to ChatGPT]: "Nature Breaking", "In Defense of Plants", "Future Ecologies", "Mongabay Newscast".
- I'm also reading this at a moment where I've been entering into some [[Lifestyle/Veganism/Veganism\|vegan]] spaces in NYC and being around people who are talking about activism and social change. I've been feeling some parallels to this in the book, especially for the concept of "direct action" -- a phrase I heard someone say, in the context of asking about opportunities for veganism-related direct action. Wikipedia says: "Direct action may include activities that can be either violent or nonviolent, targeting people, groups, institutions, actions, or property that its participants deem objectionable." Obviously way more extreme than I hope any vegan activists are doing - but an example of direct action in the book is the scene where the 2500 political leaders / influential / wealthy people were held hostage at their convention and forced to watch "propaganda" essentially trying to sway them to be better people. This was another great scene in the book. It was told from the perspective of one of the convention attendees, which was an infuriating perspective to read from - genius move by KSR.
