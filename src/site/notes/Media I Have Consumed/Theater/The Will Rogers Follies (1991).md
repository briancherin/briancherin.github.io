Broadway musical from 1991 that won the Tony awards for Best Musical and Best Original Score. It's about the life of historical figure Will Rogers (1879-1935), who was a performer and social commentator.

I chanced upon it in August 2026 due to a piano sheet music book I was working through (unsurprisingly, it's a Broadway songbook) and the song "The Big Time" caught my interest. I searched it on Youtube and saw that there was a full recording of the show (with original cast) -- can be found with a quick search. So I watched it. 

I enjoyed it! I understand what it won the awards. It has the classic extravagance of Broadway^[which normally I am not a huge fan of, but either it's grown on me, or it's just that it's expected of the time period this was made in and so I appreciate it for what it was in its context] and some very fun traits -- it kind of has a show-within-a-show gimmick, and the fourth wall is broken a bunch of times, and it's funny, and (somewhat) touching at times. There are some pretty catchy songs (especially "The Big Time", which is echoed as a recurring motif throughout the show). I've learned more about Will Rogers than I ever would have expected to, especially considering I had never heard of him before this. I don't particularly intend to learn any more about him, though, beyond being curious how accurate the show was to his actual life.

Less positive feedback about the show:
- It was a bit slow at times
- Not a particularly compelling plot, especially if you've never heard of Will Rogers
- Perhaps one too many songs about his wife missing him (though, it's an important characteristic, so I forgive it)

(Still, I think it was very well done and was entertaining to watch overall).

The writer of the musical (the book, specifically) was Peter Stone, who also wrote the show *1776* - which I have't seen^[admittedly I watched the first few minutes, years ago, and got a bit bored and haven't gone back to it since], but I've heard of and have been meaning to watch for a while.

I vaguely do want to watch (if available) every Best-Musical-winning show, so this checks off one of that list at least. Funnily, I didn't realize it had won that award until I was partway into watching the recording and was already getting absorbed into it. So when I saw the award, it was an "aha, that explains it" moment.
