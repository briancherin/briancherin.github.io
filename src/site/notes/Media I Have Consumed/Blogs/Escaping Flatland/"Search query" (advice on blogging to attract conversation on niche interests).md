- Distribution
	- Suggests not being afraid to try to get the attention of bigger bloggers to your posts, because if they re-share it out (the article is pretty heavily using Twitter as an example), it will reach a lot of people. 
	- "The way messages spread on the internet is by flowing up this order of streams, from people with smaller networks to those with larger, and then it spreads back down through the larger networks."
		- Kind of an unexpected parallel, but this is making me think of an aspect of social relationships/networking. Everybody who has social relationships is also one of these streams. Some people are large connectors -- they cultivate many relationships and know a lot of people and host a lot of events. They have a large reach. Someone who is new to a social sphere might encounter one of these large social connectors and start to enter that person's social river. By wading through this river (e.g. attending an event hosted by the social connector), they can meet other people, who each will have their own streams (some larger, some smaller), and the person can choose (if they are given the opportunity) to explore those tributaries too.
			- "Going over land, from one tributary to another, is harder than going up the stream order and then down again." --> And the parallel here is that when exploring a social network, the easiest thing to do is to keep meeting people through the people you already know, as opposed to just trying to meet people in the wild from scratch every time.
			- I am able to call out this parallel because this is how I developed my social life after moving to NYC. I am often expanding this network by meeting new people through existing social connections. Though, I also find it very fun to meet new people from scratch ("going over land"). I never know where those "streams" might lead, and what interesting people I meet through them, so it's very fun.
