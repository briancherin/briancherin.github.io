Released November 1, 2017

Notes I took while listening:
- Having a blog - putting things down on a page helps you think through things (related to some of my recent [[Meta/On Blogging/Contemplations on Blogging\|Contemplations on Blogging]]). "I write it for myself" and might as well publish for others. Write for yourself first and then for the others (or just for yourself is fine too)
- Having low expectation, "just wanted 100 people" to use it. Easy to get 100, you just go door to door / hustle. 
- Not having everything figured out shouldn't stop you from making something
- "Side projects are stupid because they have to be". **Keep it simple and stupid / low expectations on purpose, and figure out as you go**. If you have to work out every little detail from the start, you're making it more complex, and losing the momentum. 
	- (I keep telling myself for [[Projects/Software/CrowdSpeare - Crowdsourced Shakespeare Performances, or More\|CrowdSpeare]], this is stupid but i love this idea)
- Find what motivates you, keep it super simple, so there's absolutely no excuse to not do it
- Someone doing something already doesn't mean you shouldn't do your own approach/take on it. Can be a good sign - validates the idea. "You (the existing product) already gave the people a taste and they want more -- now I'll do the same thing but with a special twist."
- Having too many excuses at the beginning drives you away from doing the things you like to do.  "Most of the things you like to do don't have an obvious path to success." 
- Better to do what's fun sometime. 
- **"Waste your ideas". Put them out as much as you can**. Don't be afraid to waste them. You'll keep getting new ideas. Don't sit on your ideas. (Validating for me wanting to [[Misc/What Was Up/November 2026\|currently]] put 4 things out). 
- Comparison to writing -- once you write, you'll get more ideas. (I've found this true for my [[Interests/Creative/Writing/Creative Writing\|creative writing]])
- Can be bad if too many users come on at once when you're just getting started. Slow growth can be better. (Makes me think of new things crashing their servers when they get a lot of traffic e.g. from a viral Reddit post)
- What are your blind spots? -- programmers tend to think that their things aren't valuable because we can build everything ourselves. 
	- (This point becomes more interesting in the new AI world where it's so much easier for someone to build something)
	- (Side note - something on my mind is how much I should absorb the [[Media I Have Consumed/Podcasts/Indie Hackers Podcast/Indie Hackers Podcast\|Indie Hackers Podcast]] advice because a lot of the thoughts and approaches may be different now in the AI world. But I feel that a lot of the fundamental advise and experiences still applies, even if the industry is changing drastically.)
- "Making a feature that we thought was really cool but no one has even used it once". It happens sometimes, it's fine. 
- When you have the security of a full time job, it's a luxury. You can launch a side project with almost zero risk. (A benefit I shouldn't take for granted)
- "If you force yourself to do it, it's probably the wrong thing". "If it stops being fun, i usually kill it (or hire someone else to do it)". 
	- Reminds me of [[Media I Have Consumed/Misc/Hank Green Tells Me To Fall Downhill\|Hank Green Tells Me To Fall Downhill]]
- A form of motivation: ppl telling you they think your idea won't work / isn't good. (Can i apply this to any of my canceled projects? (e.g. [[Projects/Software/Grocery Receipt Tracker\|Grocery Receipt Tracker]]) e.g. did I give up to easily rather than being motivated by challenges?)
- If people are passionate about your idea, even if negative, it might mean there's something good to your idea
- Don't be afraid to push the status quo, to push people's buttons with your idea 
- Be an oversharer. Put your projects out there, even just talk about them or share demos online. 
	- (Digital garden. Though, maybe expand reach through Twitter. Similar thoughts in [[Media I Have Consumed/Podcasts/Indie Hackers Podcast/Pieter Levels of Nomad List - Indie Hackers Ep. 043\|Pieter Levels of Nomad List - Indie Hackers Ep. 043]])
- If you're quiet, it's hard to get noticed. 
- (On email newsletters/blogs)
	- Every email list has started with one subscriber
	- Consistency is important. Publishing once a week is better than doing it in bigger bursts less frequently.
