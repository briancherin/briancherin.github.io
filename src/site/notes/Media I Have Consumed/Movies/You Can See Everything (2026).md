I'm a big fan of Nathan Fielder's work, so I was excited to see this movie. I came out of the theater feeling pretty happy about the film and I liked the feeling of the tension that it left at the end. Afterwards, though, I learned more about Elizabeth Holmes and this totally changed my perception of the film. This shift is rooted me in barely knowing about the details of the Holmes case prior to watching the film (I knew the barest of details from reading the Wikipedia summary).

Specifically what changed my perspective was by listening to the three-part series about the movie in the *Psychology In Seattle* podcast (part [1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hyq3JbSeJ24), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wspS8gx50nM), [3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U9ltX1aVhtk)). I even paid $5 for a month of their Patreon so I could unlock the bonus parts of the episodes.

More on my reaction below but I will not hide spoilers related to the movie.

Going in, I knew the basic story, that the company's product didn't work and that they defrauded investors. I didn't remember at all ever hearing about the situation prior (I was a teenager, if not pre-teen, when it all was happening). I came in with the assumption that the downfall of the company was justified.

And the movie had the effect on me that the podcasters on *Psychology of Seattle* were worried about. The movie left me questioning: wait, maybe she actually was wrongfully sentenced, and she is just misunderstood.

Thankfully, the podcast wiped that question from my mind. She absolutely was in the wrongdoing and she was convicted of this by a jury. In reality, there is so much evidence of wrongdoing that it totally changes the interpretation of the film. Instead of, "wow nathan fielder is showing that she may not be so bad after all", it's more like, "oh wow nathan fielder is showing how much of a pathological liar+manipulator she really is by showing that she can not acknowledge anything about what was proven in court".

The podcasters are upset that the film doesn't offer more of the evidence of how she was an actual convicted liar. I agree that this is not good. They had me fooled, so probably many other people were fooled too (I mean people who didn't really know the full story going in). I don't mean "fooled" to say that Nathan Fielder is being malicious, but something was definitely left out. It's kind of upsetting actually, to feel like I was duped a little. As the podcasters say, it's a person's own fault if they are not informed on the real situation. But should there not be some responsibility in the filmmakers as well?^[one could also ponder whether liz/billy had influence over what made it into the film, but I don't really want to talk through possible theories like that here]

Still though, though I have this additional perspective on the film thanks to learning more of the backstory, my original positive perception of the film is still intact. I think Nathan did a good job of creating a fascinating documentary with many dimensions. It was three hours long and I enjoyed all of it and I don't think it should have been shorter. I was so excited in my seat after they brought Amanda Seyfried in (I didn't know her or the show Dropout prior to the film) and realized they were making her a central part of the documentary. It's a classic Nathan Fielder move. (Another classic Nathan Fielder move was the Rehearsel-esque reenactment aspect.) 

It's also really interesting how he kind of portrays himself getting swayed in some way by Liz. Or is he? The moment at the end when Amanda is questioning Nathan about whether he might be trying to soften commentary against Liz, and Nathan says "It's complicated", is really interesting, and yeah that's the earlier tension I mentioned about leaving the film. It really is complicated. I think I interpreted that line as, Nathan is kind of portraying his own act / his own character, but also possibly he is feeling real emotions of companionship with Liz due to spending so much time together and watching something of an emotional journey, but also acknowledging that he might be being swayed by her manipulative charm, but also that this is maybe part of the film's point to demonstrate her charming and manipulative action, and if so, does she realize that he's kind of fooling her to reveal herself in that way.

To summarize: Phenomenal, but possibly irresponsible, instance of filmmaking; additional context changes everything. There's a whole journey in watching this film and learning the context -- I got my bang for the buck.
